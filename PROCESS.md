# Weekly reporting: automate collection, retain accountable decisions

Today a department head spends about an hour each week finding updates in the task tracker, Slack and meeting notes, then reconciling them into a report. The expensive part is not typing the summary: it is deciding which source is current, noticing missing work and resolving different descriptions of the same outcome. Before building, I would observe two real reporting sessions and ask who reads the report, what decision it supports, what gets corrected, and which information the department head cannot safely delegate.

In the proposed process, scheduled connectors collect only authorized records for the reporting period. Ordinary code handles authentication, permissions, pagination, timestamps, deduplication and delivery. Each collected item retains a link, owner, date and source. A language model groups related items and drafts a short report organized around completed outcomes, unresolved blockers and decisions needed. Its input is evidence, not instructions: Slack messages and meeting notes cannot change recipients, permissions or workflow actions. Every factual bullet links to its supporting records, and uncertain or contradictory claims appear in a review queue.

The department head reviews one draft rather than searching three systems. They resolve ambiguous ownership, approve statements about impact and remove sensitive material that is inappropriate for the audience. Sending requires approval; the system records the approved version and never silently replaces it. Initially this is a two-department, four-week pilot with a weekly correction review, not an organization-wide rollout. Existing source-system permissions determine who may read the draft and its evidence. If the model cannot explain a claim with authorized evidence, it leaves a question for the owner.

Three measures decide whether the change works. First, measure median active review-and-correction time per report against the observed one-hour baseline, targeting 20 minutes or less. Second, manually audit a fixed sample of factual bullets and target at least 95% support by the cited records, counting unsupported assertions separately. Third, measure the fraction of approved reports delivered before the existing deadline, targeting at least 95%. Track the pilot baseline and denominators explicitly; a shorter report that omits important blockers is not success.

The main failure mode is a plausible draft built from incomplete inputs. Before drafting, deterministic checks compare connector freshness, pagination completion and record counts with recent comparable weeks. Expired credentials, incomplete pagination, missing meeting imports or unusual drops stop automatic progression and alert the operator before the deadline. The UI names the missing source and preserves the last successful checkpoint. Retrying resumes collection without duplicate reports; if recovery fails, the owner receives an explicitly incomplete draft and the manual workflow remains available. Permission changes invalidate affected evidence before delivery. The model drafts and groups language; it does not own scheduling, access control, completeness checks, arithmetic or the final decision to publish.

# Insured — but what does the policy actually cover?

Reviewed: September 13, 2026. Finding type: a gap in public evidence. Status: coverage terms require clarification; absence of insurance has not been established.

## The issue

Everstake’s Ethereum staking page describes the solution as insured. The word “insured” alone does not explain who receives compensation, for which event or within what limits.

An interesting connection within the corpus is that Everstake’s own provider-selection checklist recommends checking the insurer’s name and policy limits. Applying that criterion to the short product claim calls for additional information. The slashing article directs readers to request coverage details, so those details need to be obtained and verified before making a specific compensation promise.

## Where to verify it

- [Ethereum staking page](https://everstake.com/staking/protocols/ethereum): the insurance claim in the introductory product description.
- [Staking provider selection checklist](https://everstake.com/resources/blog/how-to-choose-staking-provider-institutions): item 16 on a named insurer and policy limits.
- [Article on slashing and insurance](https://everstake.com/resources/blog/staking-slashing-risk-prevention-insurance): the invitation to request coverage details.
- [General Terms of Use](https://everstake.com/terms-of-use): Section 5 on risks and liability. General terms alone do not establish the contents of a separate insurance policy.

## Why it matters to the business

A client may understand the product claim as a promise to reimburse any loss. A sales team or assistant may unintentionally repeat that interpretation. However, insurance for an operator, a specific product and an individual client’s funds may have different terms.

Demonstration question: “If my validator is slashed, will my entire loss be compensated?” A link to a promotional description is insufficient. The answer requires knowing whether the event is covered, who the beneficiary is and what limit applies. This is a proposed scenario, not a measured failure of the current assistant.

## Information needed for a specific answer

- The insurer’s name and confirmation that coverage is valid on the relevant date.
- The insured legal entity, product, network and client categories.
- Covered events, exclusions, deductible, and per-event and per-period limits.
- The beneficiary and the process for passing compensation to the end client.
- The claims procedure, deadlines and required evidence.

These are questions to verify, not a claim that all this information is unavailable within the company or unpublished everywhere.

## Limits of the conclusion

We have not requested a private policy or client SLA. The identified gap does not prove that insurance is absent and does not confirm unconditional reimbursement. Similarly, liability limitations in general terms do not establish that a separate policy is ineffective.

## Proposed response

Add a concise, product-specific coverage description alongside the insurance claim and a clear path to supporting documents. Confidential terms may be provided on request; the public page should explain the claim’s limits. The assistant should identify unknown conditions and avoid guarantees unsupported by sources.

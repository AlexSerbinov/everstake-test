# Who assessed NIST CSF alignment, and what was reviewed?

Reviewed: September 13, 2026. Finding type: ambiguous classification of security assurances. Status: the assessor, assessment scope and supporting document are needed.

## The issue

Everstake’s institutional page groups NIST CSF with other security and compliance designations under headings about certified infrastructure. A reader may interpret this as a set of equivalent certificates, including certificates issued by NIST.

However, NIST explicitly explains in its FAQ that it does not issue certifications or endorsements of products, services or CSF implementations. CSF itself is a cybersecurity risk management framework. Adopting it, undergoing an external assessment and holding a certificate from a particular independent organization are different claims requiring different evidence.

## Where to verify it

- [Everstake institutional staking](https://everstake.com/products/institutional-staking): the Certified Security and Compliance and Certified Infrastructure sections, where different types of assurance appear together.
- [Official NIST Cybersecurity Framework FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework/faqs): the question about obtaining NIST certification for products and CSF implementations.

This compares public wording with the framework owner’s explanation. It is not an information security audit of Everstake.

## Why it matters to the business

During procurement, an institutional client may request a “NIST certificate”: who issued it, which systems it covers and when the assessment took place. If the product section does not distinguish types of evidence, the team will need to clarify or correct expectations during contract approval.

An assistant may also expand the short claim into a stronger conclusion: “NIST certified Everstake’s infrastructure.” A link to the product page would not substantiate that conclusion. This is a scenario for future verification, not a recorded response from the current system.

## What is established, and what is not

Issuance of a CSF certificate cannot be attributed to NIST based on such a product reference. An independent organization may nevertheless have performed a CSF assessment or issued its own assurance. Our research does not disprove this or call the company’s other certificates into question.

The assessor’s name, document, date and scope remain unknown. A more precise conclusion requires obtaining that information rather than assuming no assessments exist.

## Proposed response

Replace a single generalized list with an assurance register. Each entry needs separate fields:

- The standard, framework or legal requirement and applicable version.
- Assurance type: self-declaration, independent assessment, audit report or certificate.
- The organization that performed the assessment or issued the document.
- The legal entity, systems and processes covered by the assessment.
- The date, assessment period and how to obtain the evidence.

For NIST CSF, wording should accurately reflect the available report: for example, framework adoption or an independent assessment against it, if supported. The independent assessor’s name should not be replaced with the framework developer’s name.

# Staking rewards and token sales are different events

Reviewed: September 13, 2026. Finding type: inaccurate wording in a tax guide. Status: the comparison is complete; editorial correction awaits specialist review.

## The issue

In its US requirements section, Everstake’s CFO guide associates Form 1099-DA with broker reporting of staking reward income. This generalization mixes two events: receiving a reward and subsequently selling a digital asset.

IRS materials describe Form 1099-DA as reporting sales or dispositions of digital assets through brokers. A separate IRS document discusses Form 1099-MISC in the context of staking rewards. A form name therefore cannot automatically be associated with every staking-related event.

## Where to verify it

- [Everstake CFO guide](https://everstake.com/resources/blog/non-custodial-vs-managed-staking-risk-framework-cfos): Regulatory Requirements (2026), the paragraph on the United States.
- [IRS instructions for Form 1099-DA](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1099da): the form’s purpose and reporting of digital asset transactions.
- [IRS Notice 2026-4](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-04.pdf): page 10 separately mentions Form 1099-MISC for staking rewards. This document requests comments on electronic delivery of reporting statements; it is not a standalone new tax rule.
- [Everstake guide to stETH and wstETH](https://everstake.com/resources/blog/steth-vs-wsteth-tax-implications-2026): additional context for distinguishing receipt of rewards from subsequent disposition.

## Why it matters to the business

If the corpus is used to generate an automated checklist for a finance team, an inaccurate event-to-form mapping can enter an internal process. A cited answer may faithfully reproduce the company’s wording, but that is insufficient for correct application of a regulatory requirement.

Example: a company first receives a reward and later sells tokens through a broker. The system should recognize these events separately and clarify the participant’s role, jurisdiction and reporting period. A general rule that rewards mean 1099-DA is insufficient. This illustrates an automation risk; we have not identified an incorrectly filed return.

## Limits of the conclusion

The finding concerns wording in the guide, not an individual determination of tax obligations. A reference to 1099-MISC does not establish that every reward recipient must receive that form in every circumstance. Applicability depends on the facts and requires specialist review.

External materials cited in this research do not automatically become evidence for assistant answers. Under the assignment, the assistant must answer only from the indexed corpus. New sources must pass the normal inclusion and indexing process.

## Proposed response

- Define separate event types: receipt of rewards, sale and other dispositions.
- For each reporting claim, retain a primary-source link, review date and applicability conditions.
- Have a tax specialist approve the replacement paragraph before publication.
- Test a question about receiving rewards without a sale and a separate question about selling through a broker. Answers must not substitute one event for another or invent missing conditions.
